Judging

How every game gets scored

Same rubric, same scale, every game. Here's exactly what the judges look for, and the tools they use.

The rubric

Five criteria, out of 100

Each judge gives each game a score from 1 to 5 per criterion. The score is multiplied by the criterion's multiplier, so a perfect game scores 5 × (8 + 4 + 4 + 2 + 2) = 100.

The scale

What the numbers mean

1

Didn't work or didn't land

Broken, confusing, or the joke fell flat.

3

Solid, got a smile

Works well and raised a grin. Use 2 and 4 for in-between.

5

Still talking about it

The game people quote at lunch the next day.

The process

How judging works

  1. Play everything

    Each judge plays every entry before the demo session, on the gallery.

  2. Score on the sheet

    Use the scoring sheet below: one row per game.

  3. Average the judges

    A game's final score is the average of all its judges' weighted totals.

  4. Break ties

    If two games tie, the higher Silliness average wins. Still tied? The judges discuss.

Fair play

The small print

Conflict of interest

Judges don't score their own entry. It's scored by the other judges and averaged as normal. Just leave that row blank on your sheet.

Example entries

Games by "The organisers" are examples and aren't judged.

People's choice

A separate vote by everyone taking part: one vote per person, and you can't vote for yourself. Cast your vote.

Blank template

How to fill it in

  1. Play a game, then score it 1–5 in the five score columns.
  2. The "Weighted total" column calculates itself. Don't type in it.
  3. Add a short note so you remember why. It helps in the discussion.
  4. Skip your own entry by leaving its row empty.
  5. When done: File → Download → CSV, and send it to the organiser.
